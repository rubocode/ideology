## Meritocracy

The notion of meritocracy has two aspects — justice and progress. Justice is about fairness.  The good needs to be encouraged and rewarded while the bad needs to be discouraged and punished. In Artificial Intelligence, we call this reinforcement learning aimed at progress. This should result in the situation becoming better over time for the whole.

However, there is a fly in the ointment. We don’t know how to judge good from bad in all circumstances even in the knowledge and presence of the absolute truth. It is even more difficult to decide on the measure of the rewards or the extent of punishment. Humans simply don’t have a perfect view of the past, present and the future for these judgements and decisions (පිරිපුන් වශයෙන් තුන් කල් දකින්න බැහැ).  It requires an omniscient God.

Justice or Karma doesn’t work in instantaneous fashion where you get rewarded or punished immediately after each thought, word or deed. That is not just how it works.   God is loving and forgiving and allows repentance, correction, growth and redemption.

A good contemporary example is Steve Jobs. He was born to Arabic parents, adopted by American parents and was a Zen Buddhist most of his life. He started the company Apple together with Steve Wozniak and Mike Markkula. Their initial products were successful and Steve Jobs was a multi millionaire in his early twenties. Inspired by the work at Xerox PARC, he pushed for a new kind of computer with a graphical user interface for personal use. His driving vision was that a personal computer would be a bicycle for the mind.

The first prototype was too expensive and the very CEO he hired convinced the board to put the breaks on Jobs. He had to quit the very company he founded as a failure. However, he was able to rebuild himself and make Apple the most valuable company on Earth. God allowed him to rebuild himself and make a lasting impact. Those who misjudged him and ridiculed him now look like fools.

It must be obvious that humans are not capable of creating a perfect or even a near perfect meritocracy. Whatever system of government we create, there will always be room for improvement. The optimist will embrace this limitless opportunity for improvement, progress and evolution.

The secret to governance is not trying to be God. We must do our part and leave our fate with the loving, just and sovereign God who has even arranged for the forgiveness of sins.
